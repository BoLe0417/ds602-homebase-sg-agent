==================================================
1. TYPES OF RENTAL
==================================================
- Common room: a bedroom in a shared flat, shares bathroom. Usually the cheapest option.
- Master room: a bedroom with its own attached bathroom. Costs more than a common room.
- Whole unit: rent the whole flat. Usually shared by a group of friends.
- HDB flat: public housing. Usually cheaper, often older, may live with the owner.
- Condo: private housing. Usually has a pool and gym, costs more.
- Student hostel / co-living: furnished, flexible, often includes utilities. Usually costs more per room.

Living with the owner ("owner-occupied"):
+ Often cheaper, house is usually well kept
- Less privacy, may have house rules (no visitors, no cooking, limits on aircon use)

3. BUDGET TIPS
==================================================
- Ask if utilities (electricity, water), Wi-Fi and aircon are included in the rent.
  If not, budget extra every month. Utilities (electricity, water, gas) are often
  around SGD 80-150 a month for a household; aircon use is the biggest cost.
- Rough monthly room rents (2026, all of Singapore): HDB common room ~SGD 800-1,300,
  HDB master room ~SGD 1,300-2,000, condo common room ~SGD 1,000-1,600,
  condo master room ~SGD 1,700-2,500. Whole units and studios usually start from ~SGD 2,500.
- Common upfront costs: security deposit (often 1 month's rent for a 1-year lease),
  first month's rent, stamp duty on the tenancy agreement, and sometimes an agent fee.
- Keep some money aside for moving, basic furniture and emergencies.
- Monthly transport cost goes up with a longer commute. Include it when comparing areas.

==================================================
4. VIEWING CHECKLIST
==================================================
- Water pressure and hot water in the shower
- Aircon works and is clean; ask when it was last serviced
- Signs of mould, leaks or insects
- Mobile signal and Wi-Fi speed
- Noise (near a main road, construction, MRT track?)
- Distance to MRT/bus stop, supermarket and food centre (walk it yourself)
- Who else lives there? How many people share the bathroom and kitchen?
- House rules: visitors, cooking, laundry, quiet hours, aircon use

==================================================
5. CONTRACT (TENANCY AGREEMENT) - THINGS TO CHECK
==================================================
- Rent, deposit amount, and when the deposit will be returned
- Lease length and what happens if you leave early
- Who pays for repairs (many leases say the tenant pays small repairs up to a fixed amount)
- Aircon servicing: who arranges and pays
- Whether utilities and Wi-Fi are included
- Notice period to end the lease
- Make sure everything agreed verbally is written in the contract
- Keep a copy of the signed contract and take photos of the room on move-in day
- Stamp duty on the tenancy agreement must be paid to IRAS: check the IRAS website for the rules

==================================================
6. RULES TO BE AWARE OF (check official websites; rules can change)
==================================================
- HDB flats and private homes have minimum rental periods. Check HDB (for HDB flats)
  and URA (for private homes) before signing a short lease.
- Tenants need a valid pass (e.g. Student's Pass) to rent.
- There are limits on how many people can live in one flat.

==================================================
7. AVOIDING SCAMS
==================================================
Red flags:
- Asked to pay a deposit before viewing the place
- "Landlord is overseas", only contact by WhatsApp, cannot do viewing
- Rent much cheaper than similar places nearby
- Pressure to pay quickly ("many people want it")
- Payment to a personal account that does not match the owner's name

Safe steps:
- Always view the place in person (or a live video call at minimum)
- If using an agent, check they are registered on the CEA public register
- Ask to see proof that the landlord owns the flat
- Never pay before signing a proper contract

==================================================
8. WHEN TO SEND THE USER TO A HUMAN / OFFICIAL SOURCE
==================================================
- Legal disputes with a landlord (deposit not returned, eviction)
- Questions about visa, pass or immigration status
- Suspected scam where money was already paid (advise contacting the police / ScamShield)
- Buying property (out of scope)
